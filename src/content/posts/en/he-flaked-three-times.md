Here's a question we hear constantly: someone cancels plans again and again, then keeps sending new invitations as if nothing happened. Are they interested or not?

The honest answer: the invitations tell you what they'd like. The cancellations tell you what they'll actually do. When the two disagree, believe the behavior.

## Why the invitation feels so convincing

Attention that arrives unpredictably is surprisingly sticky. Psychologists call it *intermittent reinforcement*: when warmth shows up on a random schedule, we tend to chase it harder, not less. It's the same reason a slot machine is harder to walk away from than a vending machine.

So if you feel more hooked on this person than their effort deserves, nothing is wrong with you. Your brain is responding to the schedule, not the substance.

> If they wanted to show up, the first three times would have been a great start.

## What to say instead of another yes

You don't owe anyone a long explanation. Something like this is enough: "I've noticed our plans keep falling through, so I'm going to pass. Take care." Clear, kind, done.

Or, as we'd put it: *Thôi, khỏi nha.*
